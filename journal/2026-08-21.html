A morning on how C++ actually gets built

Watched

Four of The Cherno's C++ series, all on the pipeline rather than the language — How C++ Works, How the C++ Compiler Works, How the C++ Linker Works, and Variables in C++. About an hour, listed with the rest in the video log.

Why these and not the language ones

This was deliberate. Lesson 1 had already told me that clang++ main.cpp is four programs in a trench coat, and I understood the sentence without really believing it. Watching someone draw the object files appearing and then being stitched together is what made it concrete. Same content, different channel — and that ordering, read it then watch it, seems to work better for me than either alone.

What stuck

The compiler and the linker have completely different jobs and completely different knowledge. The compiler works on one translation unit at a time and will happily accept a function it has only ever seen declared — a promise that the thing exists somewhere. The linker is the one that has to make good on the promise, and it is the only stage that can tell you nobody ever wrote the function.

I did not fully appreciate that until Monday, when I broke exactly this and got an error naming the one file that was fine. That is in the 24 August entry.
